## Leaf Structure:

*Cuticle* - layer of wax that keeps water in the leaf
*Upper epidermis* - flat layer of cells comprising the top layer of the leaf
*Lower epidermis* - bottom skin of the leaf, many pores and stomata allow gases in and out
*Stomata* - pores to let gases diffuse in and out of the leaf
*Xylem* - transports water into the leaf from the roots
*Phloem* - transports sugars out of the leaf and to the rest of the plant
*Palisade mesophyll* - the tissue that carries out most photosynthesis. It receives the most light and lets little through
*Spongy mesophyll* - loosely packed cells with air gaps to allow gas exchange. Cells are covered in a lager of moisture to aid diffusion

## Testing a leaf for starch:

1. Place a leaf in boiling water for 1 minute
2. Place the leaf in a test tube of ethanol for 10 minutes
3. Return the leaf to the hot water for another minute
4. Spread the leaf on a spotting tile and cover it with iodine solution

If starch is present, the leaf will turn blue/black. This indicates that the leaf was photosynthesising.

## Light intensity and photosynthesis in pondweed:

1. Set up a test tube rack containing a boiling tube 5cm away from a light source
2. Fill the boiling tube with sodium carbonate solution
3. Place a piece of pondweed upside-down in the boiling tube
4. Leave the pondweed for 5 minutes to allow it to produce bubbles at a regular rate
5. Start the stopwatch and record the number of gas bubbles produced by the pondweed in 1 minute
6. Repeat at varying distances from the light source

You should observe that at higher light intensities, there are more bubbles (of oxygen) produced.

## Transpiration Stream:

Over the day, stomata in the plant open and close based on external factors such as humidity, light levels and temperature. 
During the day, they open to allow gas exchange for photosynthesis. A side effect of this is water loss due to transpiration. Water is drawn up through the roots and then evaporates off of the leaves.
At night, the stomata close as there is no reason for photosynthesis and transpiration to occur.

*Translocation* - the bi-directional transfer of nutrients and minerals through the phloem
## Plant Defences:

Plant defences are grouped into 3 types: physical, chemical and mechanical.

Physical:
- thorns - directly on the stem
- spines - adapted leaves
- hairs (trichomes)
- tough waxy cuticle
- cellulose cell wall

Chemical:
- cirticaria - causes irritation
- capseican - spicy
- oxalic acid
- produces antibodies
- tastes bad

Mechanical:
 - leaf drop
 - mimicry
 - wilting - makes leaves look unattractive to herbivores

## Plant Responses:

#### Geo/Gravitropism:
--> the shoot grows away from gravity and the roots towards it
--> this happens because of each part of the plant's response to auxin. In the shoot, high auxin concentrations cause cell elongation and bend upwards away from the ground. In the root, auxin prevents cell elongation, making it bend downwards towards gravity.

![[Pasted image 20261110103806.png]]
#### Phototropism:
--> the shoot grows towards sunlight
--> light "pushes" the auxin away, causing the shoot to bend towards the light

## Plant Hormones:

Auxin:
- selective weedkillers
- rooting powder
- cloning

Gibberellin:
- increase fruit size
- end seed dormancy
- increase flowering

## Plant Diseases:

Rose black spot fungus:
--> caused my a fungus infecting the rose family
--> it infects leaves, causing black/purple spots which inhibit the plant's ability to photosynthesise
--> it is transmitted by air or water but is treatable using fungicides and destroying effected matter

Tobacco mosaic virus:
--> infects tobacco, tomatoes, potatoes and other similar plants
--> transmitted by contact
--> infects chloroplasts
--> untreatable
--> its symptoms are yellowing leaves and crinkling/curling
