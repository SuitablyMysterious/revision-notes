## Parts of the river:

*Drainage basin* - an area of land drained by a river
*Watershed* - line separating 2 drainage basins
*Tributary* - smaller river joining a larger one
*Confluence* - where 2 rivers join
*Source* - where a river begins
*Mouth* - where a river flows into the sea or a lake

## How the river changes from source to mouth?

*Discharge* - amount of water flowing ($m^3/s$)
*Width* - how wide the channel is ($m$)
*Depth* - how deep the channel is ($m$)
*Load* - material carried by the river

## Erosion:

*Hydraulic action* - water and air is forced into cracks, widening them
*Abrasion* - river load is smashed against the bed and banks
*Solution* - slightly acidic water dissolves the rock
*Attrition* - load is smashed together, smoothing and rounding it
*Vertical erosion* - erosion that cuts downwards into the rock
*Lateral erosion* - erosion that widens the river bed

## Transport:

*Traction* - larger rocks roll along the riverbed
*Suspension* - small particles are suspended in the water
*Saltation* - small pebbles bounce along the riverbed
*Solution* - dissolved substances are carried down the river

#### Floodplains:
--> formed by multiple floods depositing silt, forming a deep deposit of alluvium
--> also formed by the migration of meanders across a valley. when they reach the edges, they erode it outwards

#### Levee:
--> ridges on the riverbank caused by deposition when floodwater flows over the bank, losing energy and depositing their load

## Why do rivers flood?

![[Pasted image 20261111103513.png]]

Many factors increase flood risk, such as:
- urban/rural
- steep/shallow valley
- lots/little vegetation
- high/low soil moisture
- high/low volume of rainfall

## Flood Defences:

*Hard engineering* - building artificial structures to try and defend areas from floodwater
*Soft engineering* - involves adapting human activities to flood risk, and allowing natural processes to deal with the rainwater

## Banbury case study:

- 50km north of Oxford
- Located on the River Cherwell, a tributary of the Thames

Effects:
- Closure of the railway station
- £12.5 million worth of damages
- 150+ homes effected

Installed Defences:
- 2.9km embankment, holding back 3 million $m^3$ of water
- 2 passive flow control structures
- Biodiversity action plane
- Raised roads
- Pumping station

Benefits (Economic, Social and Environmental):
- Improved QOL
- Less disruption to education
- Saved an estimate of £100 million
- Increased biodiversity

Costs (Economic, Social and Environmental):
- Cost £18.5 million
- 100,000 tonnes of local soil was displaced and used in the defences
